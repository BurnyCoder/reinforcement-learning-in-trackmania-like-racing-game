# Independent audit of methods, results and claims (2026-11-22)

**Project:** RacingMania. **Author of the project:** Libor "Burny" Burian. **Audit by:** Claude Code, on request, as an independent pass: earlier fact-checks (`reports/source-ledger.md`, `reports/ledger/`, the 2026-11-15 amendment) were not used as evidence. **Training state:** paused since 2026-11-14; no model was trained or run for this audit.

## Verdict

No error was found that invalidates a reported result. The hypothesis evaluation is implemented as preregistered (section 5, section 6 and amendment 1 of `reports/preregistration.md`): with one seed per configuration all three hypotheses are correctly reported as **not yet tested**, and the preliminary readings are labelled as track-resampling-only intervals, not tests. Every headline number recomputed independently from the per-track result files matches the generated reports.

The problems found were: one preregistered procedure stated less precisely than it was implemented (the scripted driver's tuning rule); two construct-validity limits that the text did not state (what H3 and H2 actually measure); one protocol deviation (exploit flags not all replayed by hand); smaller reporting gaps (numerical evaluation noise, the unfiltered test set, single-run campaign comparisons, the probability of improvement with one seed); imprecise physics statements; 17 citation details; and a non-deterministic first report build. All are fixed or recorded below.

## Statistics and hypothesis evaluation: verified

- **Intervals.** Seeds and tracks are resampled independently (Owen's pigeonhole bootstrap); one multinomial draw of track counts per replicate is shared by both agents of a difference, so differences are paired on tracks. With one seed the interval reflects track sampling only, and every table and sentence says so.
- **Tests.** H1: one-sided one-sample t-test of per-seed SWS against 1.0. H2, H3: one-sided Welch test of `μa − μb > 0.10`, implemented by shifting the second sample by 0.10, which leaves its variance unchanged. Holm's step-down formula is correct; hypotheses without a p-value count in `m`, which is conservative.
- **Decision rule.** Refuted if the interval's upper end is below the threshold, else confirmed if the Holm-adjusted p is below 0.05, else inconclusive; "not yet tested" until 10 generalist, 5 blind and 5 single-track seeds have test results. This matches section 5 and amendment 1. The pilot seed-0 runs are excluded from the confirmatory configurations, as section 7 requires.
- **Cross-checks.** IQM is `scipy.stats.trim_mean(·, 0.25, axis=None)`, as rliable's `aggregate_iqm`; the probability of improvement is rliable's per-task Mann-Whitney share averaged over tracks.
- **Variance components.** The ANOVA estimators of the two-way crossed random-effects model without replication follow from E[MS_seed] = σ²_res + T·σ²_seed and E[MS_track] = σ²_res + S·σ²_track.
- **Training code.** GAE (CleanRL form); truncated episodes bootstrapped with γ·V(final observation) (Gymnasium's rule); at a track swap the rollout's bootstrap value is computed from the pre-swap state before cars are reset, so cut episodes are bootstrapped correctly; KL-adaptive learning rate with rsl_rl's rule, applied per minibatch; Schulman's k3 KL estimator; CleanRL's orthogonal initialisation; observation clipping at ±10 standard deviations. Parameter counts (108,550 + 107,265) and the 160-number observation (20 points from the nearest sample to 114 m ahead) match the code.

## Findings and actions

| # | Finding | Severity | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scripted-driver tuning rule.** Preregistration section 7 says the two parameters are tuned "to minimise its own mean time while finishing". The code, written before any track set existed (commit `6eae79a`), ranks settings by the share of validation tracks finished at all three speed scales and only then by mean time among settings within one percentage point. This rule chose the most cautious cornering setting of the grid, so the reference is slower, and H1 (beating this driver) likely easier to pass, than with a speed-first rule. It was disclosed that the driver is cautious, but not why. | Medium (reporting of a preregistered procedure) | Clarification row in the preregistration and the plan; rule stated in the README's scripted-driver section and the paper's limitations |
| 2 | **What H3 measures.** H3 compares the generalist with a blind agent *trained* without the look-ahead. A positive result shows that the input raises the score the recipe reaches; it does not show how the trained generalist uses it at inference. An inference-time input ablation of the trained policy would be the direct test (not run here: it needs model inference). | Medium (construct validity) | Stated in the README findings, the paper's limitations, the glossary and the preregistration row |
| 3 | **What H2 measures.** H2 compares the two ends of the size range (1 track, 48,949 tracks); with one seed, "1 track" is one particular generated track. The intermediate sizes are postponed. | Low–medium | Stated in the README findings and the paper's limitations |
| 4 | **Exploit flags not all replayed.** Section 8 and its amendment require every flag to be confirmed by hand. Only the pilot generalist's three flags were replayed. All 50 flagged rows are in runs that did not finish, so none gained time. | Low–medium (protocol deviation) | Recorded as a deviation in the preregistration; already disclosed in the README and `docs/physics.md`. Replay them before the exploit analysis is called final |
| 5 | **Numerical evaluation noise is not in the intervals.** About 6 % of tracks flip between finish and fall with float32 rounding differences (58 of 1,000, `reports/plan.md`); the bootstrap treats each track's outcome as fixed. | Low | Paper limitation added |
| 6 | **CEM horizon.** The decision to re-tune the CEM horizon followed its failure on the campaign and 25 test tracks; the new horizon was then chosen on validation tracks. Disclosed in the plan, not in the paper. CEM is an exploratory planning reference. | Low | Paper limitation added; plan's original row annotated |
| 7 | **Unfiltered test set.** Its 200 seeds all have a scripted reference time: the five tracks certification removed failed only at 0.9× or 1.1× speed. The check therefore says nothing about tracks the scripted driver cannot drive at all (an agent could not score on such a track, having no reference). | Low | Stated in the README track-set table and the paper's limitations |
| 8 | **Probability of improvement with one seed** is close to a per-track win share; e.g. 0.60 against the 1-track agent, whose finish rate is 45 pp lower, because on the tracks both finish the 1-track agent is usually faster. | Low (interpretation) | Note under every cross-check table |
| 9 | **Campaign comparisons** ("the generalist was 2.8 % slower than the specialist") are single runs without intervals. | Low | Sentences now give the seeds and say so |
| 10 | **Physics statements.** (a) "Straight-line braking is not limited by grip": the code skips the grip clamp only for a wheel whose side impulse is exactly zero (Rapier's 0.35.0 changelog describes the same condition), and with the configured brake (about 1.2 g) braking is below the forward grip limit (about 3 g) on flat ground anyway. (b) "The side impulse removes only 20 % of the sideways velocity": that is per wheel; the 4.5 g grip limit is reached with about 0.55 m/s of sideways slip. | Low (precision) | `docs/physics.md`, README, paper and literature review corrected |
| 11 | **Untested causal statement.** The notes called the discount factor "a likely cause" of the fast-into-corners failures; nothing tests that yet. | Low | Reworded as an untested explanation |
| 12 | **Non-deterministic first build.** Track features were used at full precision on the build that first wrote `results/tracks/*_features.csv` and rounded afterwards, so `tests/unit/test_report.py::test_build_is_idempotent` failed (it also failed on `main`). The committed outputs were not affected, because the CSVs already existed. | Low (code) | `report/features.py` now uses the cached, rounded values on the first build too; test passes |
