## Missed in the review

1. **No cited project uses progress with nothing else, and GT Sophy tested the alternatives.** Fig. 2c: without the off-course penalty, 115.07 s with 90 % of laps inside the course against 114.47 s with 98 %; a "Fixed step cost" reward is listed as DNF. A track with no walls still needs a rule for leaving the road; GT Sophy masks progress while off course and adds a penalty proportional to speed squared.
2. **Look-ahead is defined by distance or time, not by a point count.** Linesight takes every 20th zone centre, and `distance_between_checkpoints = 0.5`, so its 40 points are about 10 m apart and reach about 400 m (arithmetic by the checker). GT Sophy scales 60 points to about 6 s of travel; Fuchs samples 1.0–2.8 s ahead; Neinders uses a fixed 15 points. "About 20 points" needs a stated spacing.
3. **Linesight gives the network per-wheel ground-contact and sliding flags and gear state**, next to the track up-vector. For tracks with jumps these are the inputs that tell the agent it is airborne.
4. **Moving starts are the norm.** Fuchs starts cars "equally distributed over the racing track with an initial speed of 100 km/h"; GT Sophy randomises track positions and start speeds and relaunches from states shortly before mistakes; Song 2023 and Kaufmann restart from stored states. Green 2026 uses one fixed start and still generalises.
5. **What produced generalisation in Green 2026.** Replacing a training track when its reward plateaus raised the score by 10.29 % (Spearman test) and 10.27 % (Kalman test) over never switching, while random switching lowered it by 2.963 %; the constrained generator gave the 2.05×. Single-track policies "appear to collapse into implicit trajectory tracking". Test tracks have six to eight gates, and the number of seeds is not stated.
6. **Song 2021 measured its random-track policy by crash ratio only.** The model was chosen by "the lowest crash ratio on the validation set" of 100 unseen tracks; crashes on the 1,000 test tracks fell from 71 to 47 with the complexity curriculum and to 26 with the safety reward added. No lap times are reported for random tracks.
7. **The record of PPO in car racing is mixed.** Fuchs: PPO "required much more training data and suffered from premature convergences"; Brunnbauer: "all advanced model-free methods fail to perform one lap in more complex maps", PPO included. The positive PPO evidence is from drones and DeepRacer. Seyde et al. also found that action penalties reduce the performance of on-off policies.
8. **`viik2k/trace` describes itself as a direct precedent.** Its GitHub description reads "GPU-parallel racing sim and RL policy that drives unseen tracks without recorded racing lines. JAX + PPO." The review cites it only for reward recipes; its code was not read here, and it should be read before section 14's statement that no such public work exists is kept.
